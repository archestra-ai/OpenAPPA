\section{Limitations and Future Agenda}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Scope of claims and TCB boundary.}
The formal guarantees of \appa{} operate within explicit boundary conditions on the threat model and TCB. First, although the model is policy-untrusted, covert timing channels from an actively malicious model~\cite{hubinger2024sleeper} remain out of scope. Second, enforcement relies on declared contract labels and registered derivation functions; sanitizer correctness resides within the TCB~\cite{schwartz2010all}, and an undeclared write-side contract admits a store-mediated laundering path (\S\ref{sec:evaluation}); requiring store-writing tools to declare sink requirements rules out the undeclared case. Third, context branching enforces trajectory isolation rather than transactional side-effect rollback~\cite{wangAgentTracesTrust2026}; external network egresses committed by a child branch before discard cannot be reverted.

\paragraph{Empirical bounds and future evaluation agenda.}
The \nolinkurl{bench-corp} benchmark suite provides tight causal isolation for mechanism probing over a controlled tool surface. However, establishing operational effectiveness across production enterprise workloads requires further research: (1)~evaluating real-world task ambiguity and diverse organizational policies; (2)~testing multi-model generalization across model families and prompting strategies; (3)~quantifying long-horizon behavior and resource overheads (latency, token consumption); and (4)~measuring human approval fatigue~\cite{akhawe2013alice} and policy authoring ergonomics in realistic deployment settings.
