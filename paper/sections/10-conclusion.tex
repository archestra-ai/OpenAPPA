\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

While traditional label-based enforcement prevents unsafe data releases, standard information flow tracking can overly restrict an agent's execution context, rendering it unable to complete subsequent benign tasks.
APPA demonstrates that this usability loss is not inevitable: acquisition decisions are evaluated prior to dispatch, restrictive reads are isolated into disposable child branches, and only policy-bounded results are merged back into the continuing trajectory.
The parent context remains untainted through label-fold locality and meet operations, while a shared event log maintains a consistent state history across execution paths.
By formalizing policy algebra, remedy enumeration, and atomic rulings, APPA establishes a structural branch lifecycle rather than relying on prompt-level mitigations.
Ultimately, APPA avoids context contamination not by attempting post-hoc sanitization, but by ensuring that the primary execution trajectory never observes the restricted data.
