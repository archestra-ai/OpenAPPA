\section{Definitions and Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

This appendix fixes the shared setting and proves the results stated in the body: the policy algebra (Propositions~\ref{prop:collapse} and~\ref{prop:descent}), branch taint confinement (Proposition~\ref{prop:preservation} and Theorem~\ref{thm:branch}), implemented Remedy completeness (Theorem~\ref{thm:remedy}), and call-scoped release (Theorem~\ref{thm:release} and Corollary~\ref{cor:export}).

\paragraph{Setting.}
A Label is a pair $(R,t)\in S=\powerset(U)\times T$, ordered by restrictiveness: $(R',t')\le(R,t)$ iff $R'\subseteq R$ and $t'\le t$ (\S\ref{sec:enforcement}). The meet is component-wise, $(R,t)\meet(R',t')=(R\cap R',\min(t,t'))$, and is the greatest lower bound, so $x\meet y\le x$, and $x\meet y=x$ iff $x\le y$ (absorption). A trajectory Label $L$ changes only by folding admitted contributions, $L'=L\meet D$. For a registered tool $\tau$ with \texttt{emits} $K_\tau$, write $d_\tau$ for the \emph{effective} contribution its admitted output would fold (\S\ref{sec:enforcement}): the declared \texttt{delta}, with a bound output sanitizer's derivation audience in place of the raw one, and with a pending-cast dimension contributing identity. The prospective Label of a call to $\tau$ at $L$ is $c_\tau(L)=L\meet d_\tau$; a call's requirement gaps are its unmet \texttt{requires}, with label requirements evaluated against $c_\tau(L)$, history predicates against the committed-effect projection $\mathcal{E}$, and attention demands per call (\S\ref{sec:model}). The dispatch rule (\S\ref{sec:enforcement}--\S\ref{sec:rulings}): a call dispatches iff it has no gaps and no unaccepted narrowing, or an atomic plan execution covers each gap with a ruling over the exact rendered call and records the agent's acceptance of any narrowing, in one indivisible step with the dispatch.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}.}
Let $L_0=(R_0,t_0)$ and let the $i$th action contribute the restrictive meet $(\cap A_i,\min r_i)$.  After $n$ actions,
\[
  \fold(a_1\cdots a_n)(L_0)
  = \left(R_0 \cap \bigcap_{i=1}^{n} A_i,
           \min\{t_0,r_1,\ldots,r_n\}\right).
\]
For $n=0$ this is the identity action.  The inductive step intersects the next reader set and takes the next minimum; associativity of intersection and minimum yields the displayed form.  Thus the fold is represented completely by the current intersection and minimum, i.e., by one running meet.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:descent}.}
Every update is $L_{i+1}=L_i\meet D_{i+1}$, and the meet is a lower bound, so $L_{i+1}\le L_i$: the trajectory Label is a descending chain in $S$, which is monotone descent. For settlement, define $\varphi(R,t)=|R|+\mathrm{rank}(t)\in\mathbb{N}$, where $\mathrm{rank}$ indexes the finite trust chain $T$ and $|R|\le|U|$ is finite. A strict descent $L_{i+1}<L_i$ strictly shrinks the reader set or strictly lowers the trust rank, so $\varphi(L_{i+1})\le\varphi(L_i)-1$. Since $\varphi\ge 0$, a trajectory performs at most $\varphi(L_0)$ strict descents---at most $|U|+|T|-1$ for $L_0\in\powerset(U)\times T$; an implementation that seeds untouched trajectories at a neutral top above the deployment chain adds one. After the last one, every further update satisfies $L_i\le D$ and leaves the Label fixed by absorption; such identity updates may continue indefinitely, which is the settlement-without-termination reading in \S\ref{sec:model}.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:preservation} and Theorem~\ref{thm:branch}.}
Let $D_1,\ldots,D_n\in S$ be the Label contributions admitted by a child after its fork.  Trajectory-local folding gives
\[
  L_c^n = L_p^{\mathrm{fork}} \meet D_1 \meet \cdots \meet D_n
          \le L_p^{\mathrm{fork}}.
\]
Because these updates apply only to $L_c$, they leave $L_p$ unchanged, and abandonment performs no parent Label transition.  A return performs the sole parent transition $L_p'=L_p\meet\lab(v)$.  The meet is a lower bound, so $L_p'\le L_p$, and the absorption law gives $L_p'=L_p$ if and only if $L_p\le\lab(v)$.  These are exactly the abandonment and return cases of Theorem~\ref{thm:branch}, making it a branch-lifecycle corollary of parent preservation.

\paragraph{Concurrency remark.}
The Label argument does not require the parent to pause: parent and child folds are disjoint trajectory-local state, so interleaving their actions does not change the transitions above.  They do share the event log, whose appends must be serialized by the host under the threat model.  Concurrency can therefore affect event order and the history observed by predicates, but not the parent-preservation argument.

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:remedy} (Implemented Remedy completeness).}
The implemented transition system is the finite abstraction the planner searches. A state is $\sigma=(L,E)$: the trajectory Label and the support $E=\mathrm{supp}\,\mathcal{E}(\ell)$ of the committed-effect projection (history predicates depend only on membership). A call $C$ on tool $\tau_C$ \emph{clears directly} at $\sigma$ iff every gap of $\tau_C$ at $\sigma$ has a registered authority whose mandate covers it---a trust floor within a trust ceiling, an \texttt{includes} within a reader ceiling, a \texttt{no\_prior} named by a waiver, an attention mark named by an \texttt{attends} mandate---and any narrowing is accepted; \texttt{prior} and cap gaps have no covering mandate. Direct clearance is exactly the existence of the atomic \texttt{Authorize}/\texttt{Accept} plan. The system steps $\sigma\to_\tau\sigma'$ iff a registered $\tau$ clears directly at $\sigma$ under the prerequisite relaxation---every \texttt{includes} gap, static or placeholder-resolved, is treated as satisfiable, an over-approximation that only adds transitions---and $\sigma'=(c_\tau(L),\,E\cup K_\tau)\ne\sigma$. $C$ is \emph{curable} at $\sigma_0$ iff $C$ clears directly at some state reachable from $\sigma_0$.

\emph{Finiteness and well-foundedness.} Reachable Labels lie in $\{L_0\meet\bigwedge_{\tau\in X}d_\tau : X\subseteq\text{tools}\}$ and reachable effect sets in $\powerset(\bigcup_\tau K_\tau)$: both finite. Along any transition the Label descends (a meet) and the effect set grows (a union), and $\sigma'\ne\sigma$ forces at least one strict change, so $\mu(\sigma)=\varphi(L)+\lvert\bigcup_\tau K_\tau\setminus E\rvert$ (with $\varphi$ from the descent proof) strictly decreases. The transition relation is therefore acyclic, and every path has length at most $\mu(\sigma_0)$.

\emph{The planner decides curability.} Unfolding the definition, $C$ is curable at $\sigma$ iff $C$ clears directly at $\sigma$ or some transition $\sigma\to_\tau\sigma'$ reaches a state where $C$ is curable. By Noetherian induction over $\mu$ this recursion is well defined and terminates, and the planner's depth-first search computes it verbatim: its revisit guard never fires on an acyclic relation, and an Unknown prospective Label is treated as terminal (the cast de-scope below). The planner emits the atomic plan iff $C$ clears directly at $\sigma_0$, and a curative \texttt{Redispatch} iff $C$ does not clear directly but some first transition reaches a curable state; \texttt{Fork} never counts (a child starts at the same Label and cures nothing, \S\ref{sec:branching}). Hence the planner returns neither a plan nor a curative recommendation iff $C$ is not curable at $\sigma_0$: the empty verdict decides unreachability over the abstraction. The implemented search is additionally cross-checked by property-based tests against an independently implemented forward-closure reference planner over randomized registries and states.

\emph{Runs are simulated.} In an in-scope engine run following the block, rulings, acceptances, plan records, boundary events, and failed or indeterminate closes are the identity on $(L,E)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:release}(ii)). A reported-success close whose value is admitted folds $d_\tau$ into $L$ and adds $K_\tau$ to $E$: it projects to the transition $\sigma\to_\tau\sigma'$, or to an erased stutter when $(L,E)$ is unchanged. An unruled dispatch has no gaps; for a ruled dispatch, atomic plan validation establishes a covering mandate for every gap and the acceptance of any narrowing, so the dispatched tool clears directly and is a fortiori runnable under the relaxed prerequisite relation. A re-proposal of $C$ dispatches only when $C$ clears directly at the current state, because \texttt{prior} and cap gaps admit no ruling. Every in-scope run in which $C$ eventually dispatches therefore projects to a path $\sigma_0\to\cdots\to\sigma_n$ with $C$ clearing directly at $\sigma_n$; contrapositively, the empty verdict proves no such run exists.

\emph{Scope bounds.} The quantification excludes: sanitizer argument substitution and compiled composites (remedy species outside the implemented planner); cast resolution of an Unknown dimension (the search treats an Unknown prospective Label as terminal); forks (never curative, Theorem~\ref{thm:branch}); and reported-success closes that withhold their value (\S\ref{sec:model}), which commit \texttt{emits} without folding the contribution and so leave the Label above the modeled successor. Runs through the excluded events remain fully checked---enforcement soundness is unaffected; only the empty verdict's coverage is bounded. The over-approximations---the \texttt{includes} relaxation, and the pending-cast identity contribution in $d_\tau$, which an out-of-scope cast resolution can only lower---run in the safe direction: they can only report curable more often, never certify an empty verdict wrongly.

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:release} (Call-scoped release).}
(i)~By the dispatch rule, a call with requirement gaps dispatches only through atomic plan execution: one indivisible step renders the call, obtains a covering ruling for each gap over that rendered call, records the acceptance of any narrowing, and dispatches. Each ruling is created and consumed within this step, so no reachable log contains a free-standing ruling, and no ruling is consumed by a second dispatch. Gaps with no covering mandate (\texttt{prior}, caps) cannot be present at any dispatch: a call carrying them dispatches only after redispatch remedies cure them and a re-check finds them absent.

(ii)~By induction on the log: among all event kinds, only value admissions, merges, and audited cast resolutions update a trajectory Label---admissions and merges are meets (\S\ref{sec:model}), and a cast only establishes an Unknown dimension, never rewrites an established one---and only reported-success closes extend $\mathcal{E}$. A ruling or acceptance event is a record, the identity on every trajectory Label and on $\mathcal{E}$. The ruled dispatch's own consequences enter only through those ordinary channels: its admitted value's validated Label folds restrictively, and its \texttt{emits} commit on reported success.

(iii)~By (i), every dispatch with an unmet release-side Label requirement consumes at least one ruling, and no ruling is shared between dispatches, so in any trajectory such releases are matched to the explicit rulings in the log: no unruled inadequate release exists. By (ii) and Proposition~\ref{prop:descent}, the Label after a ruled dispatch is at most the Label before it, so the same resolved release-side requirement---a trust floor, or an \texttt{includes} over the same resolved recipient set---that failed before the override still fails after it (both are monotone in the Label; a later rendered call whose placeholders resolve differently presents a different requirement, checked afresh). A cap or a \texttt{prior} predicate may newly pass after the override, but not through the ruling: the reader set only shrank by the trajectory's own restrictive descent, and the effect is one the ruled call was itself authorized to commit. An override therefore widens no later Label and confers no authorization on any other dispatch: each subsequent inadequate release presents its own gaps and, by (i), consumes its own rulings.

\begin{corollary}[Child-value export]\label{cor:export}
Suppose a child returns $v$ and the parent accepts the merge. Then every later rendered parent call whose trust floor or resolved recipient set fails against $\lab(v)$ also fails against its own prospective Label, so its dispatch consumes a fresh explicit ruling; and no ruling consumed inside the child can serve as that ruling.
\end{corollary}
After the merge, $L_p'=L_p\meet\lab(v)\le\lab(v)$, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:descent} every later parent Label---hence every later prospective Label---stays below $\lab(v)$. Trust floors and \texttt{includes} over a fixed resolved recipient set are monotone in the Label, so a requirement failing against $\lab(v)$ fails against any Label below it, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:release}(i) the dispatch consumes a fresh ruling. A ruling is consumed by the single rendered dispatch it names (Theorem~\ref{thm:release}(i)), a child ruling names a child dispatch, and pending rulings and plan offers do not survive the fork boundary (\S\ref{sec:branching}), so no child ruling covers a parent dispatch. Caps are source-context bounds and antimonotone; as in \S\ref{sec:enforcement}, they fall outside this release-side guarantee.
